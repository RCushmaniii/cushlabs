You look up your business on Google Maps and there it is: a one-star review from three weeks ago, with no reply.

You didn't see it. Nobody on your team saw it. But everyone who searched for your business since then did.

Replying to reviews is one of those jobs every owner knows matters and almost nobody does on time. Not out of laziness. You're running the business. This guide gives you copy-ready replies, the rules worth following, and the things you should never write, so a reply takes two minutes instead of half an afternoon.

## Why replying matters

A review isn't a conversation between you and one customer. It's a conversation between you and one customer **in front of everyone deciding whether to walk in.**

The person who left the review already had the experience. The real reader of your reply is the next customer, the one comparing your salon with the one down the street, or your dental office with three others in the same plaza. A calm, kind reply to a complaint says more about your business than twenty unanswered five-star reviews.

Google says it plainly in its help center: [replying to reviews](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474050?hl=en) shows customers you value their feedback. Google recommends replying. I won't promise you that replying moves you up the map, because nobody outside Google can honestly promise that. What I can tell you is that a profile where the owner answers looks cared for, and one where nobody answers looks abandoned.

## Rules before you reply

**Fast, but not angry.** Aim for a day or two. A negative review answered a week later looks careless; one answered ten minutes later, while you're fuming, looks worse. If it stung, wait an hour.

**Use the person's name.** "Thanks, Sarah" reads differently from "Dear valued customer." Google's own [review tips](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122?hl=en) suggest addressing the reviewer by name and signing off with your name or initials.

**No copy and paste.** If your last ten replies all say "Thanks for your business!", readers stop believing a person writes them. Mention something specific: the haircut, the cleaning, the dress, the brisket.

**Short.** Three or four lines. When a reply runs long, nobody gets to the point.

**Never reveal customer details.** Not their phone number, what they bought, when they came in, or what treatment they had. For clinics and medical or dental offices this is even more serious: **don't even confirm the person is your patient.** Write about how you work, not about their case. Health details in a public reply are a trust problem, and in the US they can be a HIPAA problem too.

## Copy-ready replies

These are templates to adapt, not to paste as-is. Change the details and write the way your business talks.

### 5-star review

**Hair salon:**

> Thank you, Sarah! So glad you love the balayage. Megan put a lot of care into getting that tone right, so I'll pass your note along. See you for the touch-up. — Lisa

**Restaurant:**

> Thanks so much, James. We're thrilled the brisket was the highlight of your Sunday. The pitmaster starts it at 4 a.m., so that means a lot. Come back soon! — The team at Hickory House

### 3-star review

**Boutique:**

> Thanks for taking the time to write, Emily. Glad you liked the new collection, and you're right that we were short-staffed at the register on Saturday. We're adding help that day. Hope you'll give us another try. — Rachel, Willow Boutique

**Dental office:**

> Thank you for the feedback, Mark. We know waiting in the lobby is frustrating, even when the visit itself goes well. We're adjusting our schedule so appointments start on time. If you'd like to talk it through, call us at (555) 010-0000. — Dr. Patel

### 1-star review (fair)

When the complaint is right, the best reply owns it, apologizes without excuses, and takes the conversation offline.

**Restaurant:**

> Anna, you're absolutely right, and we're sorry. An hour wait for your food isn't what we want for anyone. The kitchen fell behind that night and we've talked it through with the team. We'd like to make it right; send us a message and we'll take care of it. — Chris, manager

**Hair salon:**

> We're so sorry the color didn't come out the way you hoped, Jessica. That's not the result we want for any client. We'd love to fix it at no charge; message us and we'll book you with our colorist. — Lisa

Notice that neither one offers anything **in exchange for changing or removing the review**. Fixing the problem is fine. Making the fix conditional on the review is not.

### 1-star review (unfair or fake)

The temptation here is to defend yourself hard. Don't. Reply calmly, stick to facts, accuse nobody, and then report it (steps below).

**Dental office:**

> Hello. We can't find a visit in our records that matches what you describe, but we take every comment seriously. If you were seen here, please call us at (555) 010-0000 so we can look into it directly. — Bright Smile Dental

**Boutique:**

> Hi Kevin, we're sorry about your experience. Our return policy is 15 days with a receipt, and it's posted in the store and on our profile. If there was a mix-up with your purchase, we're happy to look into it; send us a message. — Willow Boutique

Look at what the dental reply **doesn't** say. It doesn't say "you are not our patient," and it gives no details about anyone. It just says the visit can't be found and opens a door.

### Rating with no text

Lots of people leave stars and nothing else. One line is enough, as long as it isn't the same line every time.

**Five stars, no text (restaurant):**

> Thanks for the five stars! We're so glad you came in. See you next time. — Hickory House

**One or two stars, no text (salon):**

> We're sorry your visit wasn't what you expected. We'd really like to know what happened so we can do better; if you can, message or call us. — Lisa

## What you should never write

- **"You're lying,"** or any accusation. Even if it's true, readers see an owner picking a fight.
- **The customer's personal details.** Phone, address, what they bought, what treatment they had, when they came in. Not even to "set the record straight."
- **"That's not our fault"** followed by a list of excuses. Explain once, without over-defending.
- **Offers in exchange for removing or changing the review.** That breaks Google's policies.
- **Sarcasm or "lol."** In writing, it doesn't read as a joke.
- **Promotions inside a reply to a complaint.** "Sorry about that! Also, half-price wings on Tuesdays" reads like you never read the complaint.

## How to report a fake review

First, an honest caveat: **you can't remove a review because you don't like it.** Google only takes down reviews that break its [content policies](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114?hl=en): spam, offensive language, reviews from someone who was never a customer, from a competitor, or from someone with a conflict of interest.

If the review falls into one of those, these are the steps Google currently documents on its [Report inappropriate reviews](https://support.google.com/business/answer/4596773?hl=en) page:

1. Go to your Business Profile (search for it on Google or Maps while signed in with the account that manages it).
2. Select **Read reviews**.
3. Next to the review, select **Report**.
4. Choose the reason that fits best (for example, spam).
5. Select **Send report**.

To track it, Google has a **Reviews Management Tool**, linked from that same help page. It shows whether a report is *decision pending*, was reviewed with *no policy violation*, or was *escalated*. If it's rejected, you get **one appeal** per review, so make it count with clear reasons.

While Google decides, reply to the review calmly, like the examples above. A fake review with a composed reply under it tells readers almost everything they need to know.

## How to ask for more reviews

Recent reviews matter to people comparing you with the competition. Asking for them is allowed. How some businesses ask is not.

**What works:**

- **A QR code at the counter, on the table, or on the receipt.** Google lets you create a short link and QR code from your Business Profile that takes customers straight to the review form.
- **A follow-up message by Messenger or text** to customers who already message you there. Something like: *"Thanks for coming in today, Sarah! If you have a minute, a Google review really helps us: [link]."* Short, after the visit, and only to people who already talk to you on that channel.
- **Asking in person.** At checkout, when the customer says "I love it," is the best moment there is.

**What breaks Google's policies:**

- Offering **discounts, gifts, payment or free services** in exchange for a review.
- **Asking only happy customers** for reviews and skipping everyone else.
- Having employees or family review your business, or giving your staff a review quota.

Google can remove reviews collected that way, and all that effort is gone. It isn't worth it.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I have to reply to every Google review?

You don't have to, but Google recommends replying because it shows customers their feedback matters. If you can't get to all of them, start with the negative ones and the ones that ask a question, then the positive reviews with text.

### Can I delete a negative review?

No. Only the person who wrote it can delete it. What you can do is reply calmly and, if the review breaks Google's policies (spam, offensive language, someone who was never a customer, a conflict of interest), report it from your Business Profile so Google reviews it.

### How long does Google take to review a reported review?

Google doesn't commit to a timeline. You can check the status in its Reviews Management Tool: decision pending, no policy violation, or escalated. If the report is rejected, you get one appeal.

### Can I offer a discount in exchange for a review?

No. Google's policies prohibit offering payment, discounts, or free goods or services in exchange for a review, and they also prohibit asking only happy customers for reviews. Ask everyone, and offer nothing in return.

### Can I let AI reply to my reviews?

You can let AI write the draft, which saves you from the blank page. But the reply goes out under your business's name, so someone who knows the business should read and approve it before it posts. That is how CushLabs works: you approve every reply.
