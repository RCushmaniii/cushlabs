A clinic owner in Guadalajara asked me last week how much WhatsApp would cost her. It is a fair question with an annoying answer, because there are two different products called WhatsApp Business and only one of them costs money.

Here is the version I wish someone had written for her.

## The free one and the paid one

**The WhatsApp Business app** is the one on your phone. Green icon, labels, quick replies, a catalogue. It is free, it will stay free, and for a lot of businesses it is genuinely enough.

**The WhatsApp Business Platform** is the one software talks to. It is what lets a system send appointment reminders on their own, or answer a customer at 11pm without you awake. Meta charges the business for messages sent through it.

Same brand, same green, completely different commercial reality. Almost every confusing conversation about "WhatsApp pricing" is really two people talking about different products.

## Meta charges per message, and the category decides the price

Meta sorts every template message a business sends into a category, and the category sets the rate:

- **Utility** — an appointment reminder, a confirmation, an order update. The cheapest.
- **Authentication** — a verification code.
- **Marketing** — a promotion, an offer, anything selling. The most expensive by a wide margin.

The important part is that **Meta decides the category, not you.** You propose one when you submit the template, and Meta's review either agrees or re-classifies it. A reminder with one promotional line in it does not get charged as a reminder with a small surcharge. It gets re-classified as marketing, and the whole message is priced as marketing.

That is not a detail for later. It is the difference between a message that costs very little and one that costs several times more, decided by a sentence someone added to sound friendly.

## What changes on 1 October 2026

Until now, **service messages** have been free. A service message is what a business sends back inside the 24-hour window after a customer writes in — the reply, the follow-up question, the answer.

From **1 October 2026**, Meta charges for them.

If you are reading this before that date, the change has not landed yet. If you are reading it after, it has, and any advice you find online that says "replies are free" was written before the change and nobody went back to update it.

This is the single most important fact for anyone deciding what to automate on WhatsApp, because it changes the economics of conversation specifically.

## Why a chat bot on WhatsApp is worse value than it sounds

Put an AI assistant in a WhatsApp inbox and it will hold conversations. That is the point of it. A conversation is six, ten, fifteen messages.

On Facebook Messenger or Instagram, that costs nothing per message. On WhatsApp, after 1 October, every one of those replies is billable — and the bill is yours, not your software provider's.

Now think about volume. An assistant that answers well gets used more. A busy month means a bigger bill, and the better the assistant performs the faster that number grows. You are paying per turn for a thing whose whole job is to keep talking.

Compare that with a reminder. **An appointment reminder is one message.** One per appointment, sent once, at a utility rate. The cost is predictable, it scales with your bookings rather than with how chatty your customers are, and every single one of those messages is doing work you can point at: the appointment happened, or it got rescheduled instead of missed.

That is why I do not put an AI chat inbox on my clients' WhatsApp numbers, and it is not a technical limitation — it is what the pricing rewards. WhatsApp is superb at getting one important message opened. Your customers open it because it is not full of noise. Filling it with chat is how it stops being that.

Conversation belongs where replies are free and where nobody minds a bot: Messenger, Instagram, your website. Reminders and confirmations belong on WhatsApp.

## The risk nobody mentions in the pricing conversation

There is a second cost that never appears on an invoice.

WhatsApp watches how people react to your messages. Blocks and reports lower a number's quality rating, and a number with poor quality gets restricted: first the ability to send templates, then the ability to send anything, then in the worst case the number is removed from the platform entirely.

That number is your business line. It is on your sign, in your Google listing, in the phone of every customer you have ever served. A chat bot that annoys people on Facebook is an annoying bot. A chat bot that annoys people on WhatsApp is a risk to the phone number your business runs on.

Every message you send there should be one the customer is glad to get. That is a content standard before it is a cost one.

## What to ask a provider before you sign anything

Four questions. The answers tell you most of what you need to know.

1. **Whose name is the WhatsApp Business Account in?** If it is not yours, you are a sub-account, and leaving is a negotiation rather than a decision.
2. **Who does Meta bill?** If Meta bills you directly at its published rate, you can open the account and read the charges. If the provider is billed and resells you messages, a markup can live in the gap and you cannot see it.
3. **What category will my templates be?** If they cannot tell you before writing them, they have not thought about your bill.
4. **What happens when Meta re-classifies one of my templates?** It happens. The right answer involves noticing and telling you, not finding out on an invoice.
